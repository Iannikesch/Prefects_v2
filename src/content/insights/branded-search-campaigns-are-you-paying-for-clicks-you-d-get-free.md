Someone searches for your company by name, clicks your ad and buys. The dashboard records a conversion and the campaign looks cheap and efficient. But that person was already looking for you. Would they have found you anyway? That's the debate over branded search advertising, and the answer is: sometimes.

## What branded campaigns are

Branded search campaigns bid on your own company name and its variations. They usually show remarkably low cost per click and strong conversion rates, since the searcher already knows who you are. That makes them look like the best campaign in the account. It also makes them the easiest to overcredit, because much of what they capture would have arrived through the organic listing sitting just below.

## Reasons to run them

There are real reasons to run them. Competitors can bid on your name, and if you're absent, their ad can sit at the top of the results for people searching for you. Paid results also take up more of the page, pushing competitors lower. You control the message, such as a current promotion or a specific landing page, and sitelinks can send people exactly where you want them. For some businesses these benefits clearly outweigh the cost.

## Reasons to question them

And there are reasons to question them. If nobody bids on your name and your organic listing ranks first, you may be paying for clicks you'd have received free. The test is whether the paid ad is incremental, meaning whether it produces conversions that wouldn't have happened without it.

## Run a pause test

The cleanest way to find out is a pause test. Turn off branded search for a defined period, say three to four weeks, and watch total conversions from search, paid and organic combined. If total conversions barely move, the paid campaign was mostly recapturing existing demand. If they drop noticeably, or a competitor shows up in your place, the campaign was doing real work. Ideally, run the test during a stable stretch without a big promotion, so other changes don't muddy the answer.

## An example

Consider a made-up case. A regional law firm spending $1,800 a month on branded search and crediting it with 41 consultation requests. During a four-week pause, organic search picks up most of the lost clicks, and total consultation requests from search fall from 41 to 37 against a comparable month. The campaign was responsible for roughly four incremental requests. At $1,800, that's $450 each, far above the $44 the dashboard had suggested.

## Read the result carefully

Don't judge the test on one month alone, for the noise reasons that apply to any small sample, and keep an eye on competitor activity. If a rival is bidding on your name, the pause may cost more than it saves, and the answer to 'should I run branded' changes. Check by searching your own name from a few different locations, or by using the auction insights report if your platform provides one, to see who else is showing up.

Whatever you decide, report branded and non-branded search separately. Blending them is how an account's efficiency gets inflated: cheap branded conversions mask expensive non-branded ones, and the average looks healthy while prospecting campaigns quietly underperform. Separate reporting also keeps everyone honest when someone says the search campaign is working.

## Keep it clean

Branded campaigns need their own hygiene. Brand terms can overlap with generic ones, and some competitor or unrelated searches can slip in through broad matching. Keep the branded campaign tightly matched to your name and its variations, add negative keywords where needed, and keep it separate from non-branded campaigns so the reporting stays clean.

If a competitor does bid on your name, the calculation changes. Their ad might appear above your organic listing, and some of your most motivated customers may click it. A modest defensive budget can make sense, particularly when the cost per click on your own name is low. The question becomes whether protecting those clicks costs less than the sales you'd lose.

Organic position matters too. If your website doesn't rank first for your own name, perhaps because a directory or review site outranks you, branded ads fill a real gap. Check what actually appears when someone searches for you. Sometimes the thing standing between a customer and your website is a listing you don't control.

Be careful with automated bidding on branded campaigns. If the system is told to maximize conversions, it will happily bid more for people who were going to convert anyway, since they convert at a high rate. Consider capping bids or using an impression-share target so the campaign protects your position without overspending to win clicks that were already yours.

## The so-what

Open your search campaign report this week and split out the branded terms. Look at what share of spend and conversions they represent, and at what the non-branded campaigns cost on their own. If branded accounts for most of your 'results', schedule a pause test and find out what that spend is actually buying.
