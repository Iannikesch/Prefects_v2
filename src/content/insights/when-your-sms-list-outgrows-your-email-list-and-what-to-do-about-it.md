A growing number of eCommerce and retail brands are finding that their SMS list is catching up to their email list, or passing it. That doesn't happen everywhere, so check your own numbers first. When it does happen, most strategies haven't caught up, still defaulting to email-first thinking out of habit.

## Why it happens

Part of it is friction. Typing an email address on a mobile checkout is annoying. Tapping one checkbox for texts, especially with a discount attached, is easy. Mobile-optimized checkouts have quietly made SMS opt-in easier to collect than email opt-in.

The other part is behavior. People read texts almost immediately. Email competes with dozens of unread messages and can sit for hours or days. SMS open rates are commonly reported above 80%, though they're hard to measure precisely and vendors report them. Email open rates are typically 20 to 40% and are inflated by Apple's Mail Privacy Protection. The gap is real even if the exact figures aren't. It's why a smaller SMS list can out-earn a larger email list.

## Where most brands go wrong

They built lifecycle marketing around email years ago and bolted SMS on later, using it mainly for flash sale blasts. That wastes what the channel does best. SMS suits short, time-sensitive, high-value messages. It doesn't suit long-form content, and treating it as a smaller version of email misses the point.

## Split the job by channel

Build a simple rule for which messages go where, so you aren't deciding fresh every campaign.

| Message | Best channel | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shipping delay or delivery update | SMS | Urgent, short |
| Restock or back-in-stock alert | SMS | Time-sensitive |
| Appointment reminder | SMS | Needs same-day attention |
| Seasonal buying guide | Email | Needs space and images |
| Brand story, education | Email | People expect to read |
| Short-window promotion | Both | SMS for the nudge, email for detail |

Most real campaigns fall in between. Having the rule agreed in advance speeds up decisions and keeps the channel strategy consistent as the team grows.

## Frequency matters more in SMS

High engagement cuts both ways. Over-messaging on SMS burns out a list far faster than on email. A brand sending five promotional texts a week will see opt-outs climb, where the same cadence by email might just get ignored.

Set a frequency cap per week, and track opt-out rate by channel every month. Treat a rising SMS opt-out trend as more urgent than a rising email unsubscribe trend. Catching it early is much easier than rebuilding a fatigued list.

## Segment before you send

Segment SMS by purchase behavior instead of treating the whole list as one audience. The same principle applies to email, but it matters more when subscribers will tolerate so few messages. A relevant text to a smaller group beats a broad blast on both conversion and long-term list health.

## SMS has its own deliverability rules

Carrier filtering is a different failure mode from email deliverability. Messages that read like spam, with heavy urgency language, all caps or excessive punctuation, can be blocked before they reach a phone, however clean your list is. Register your sending number, follow carrier guidelines, and keep copy plain. Don't assume email best practices carry over.

## What to do with a growing SMS list this month

Start with housekeeping. Check that every subscriber has clear, recorded consent, that STOP works, and that your number is registered. Then audit your current texts against the table above and move any long messages to email.

Next, add one behavior-triggered text. A back-in-stock alert or a shipping update is the easiest, because the customer asked for it and the message is short. Triggered messages tend to earn more per send than broadcast blasts, since each one lands when the person actually needs it. Measure revenue per message sent for the triggered text against your last broadcast, and use the result to decide what to build next.

## The so-what

Pull your list sizes and your revenue per subscriber for each channel. If SMS has caught up, move time-sensitive messages there, keep long content in email, set a weekly cap, and watch opt-outs monthly. Then test: send the same offer to matched groups on each channel and compare revenue per recipient before you move more budget.
