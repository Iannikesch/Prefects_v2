Every agency pitch deck has a slide about data. Very few show what that means day to day. Some agencies mention analytics in meetings. Others build their whole process around proving what worked. Most businesses find out which kind they hired about eighteen months and a lot of invoices later.

## One question that sorts them fast

Ask your current agency, or the one you're about to sign: which channel drove your last ten closed customers?

Skip impressions and clicks. You want customers who paid you money. If the answer takes more than a day to produce, or comes back as "brand awareness plus some paid search," you're looking at a vibes-based operation in a data costume.

An agency running on vibes may not be lying. They believe the creative is working because engagement looks good and the client seems happy. But engagement and revenue are different things. A campaign can collect thousands of likes and zero new customers, and nobody notices until the budget conversation gets uncomfortable.

## What a data-driven shop does differently

It starts with the customer and works backward. Where did this person come from? What did they see before they converted? How many touches happened between the first ad and the signed contract?

That takes real plumbing: tracking that holds up as cookies fade, a CRM that talks to your ad platforms, and someone whose job is reconciling the two when they disagree. They will disagree.

None of this is exotic. What's rare is an agency willing to build it before spending your money, because the plumbing is slow and it doesn't make a good highlight reel. A polished video and a chart of impressions are easier to show than a slightly messy attribution report with honest gaps in it.

## How they handle a soft month

Listen to how an agency explains bad numbers. A vibes-driven shop reaches for stories: the market was soft, the audience needs more exposure, brand building takes time. Sometimes that's true.

A data-driven agency treats a soft number as a question. It comes back with a specific hypothesis about what changed and a test to check it.

## Try this before your next meeting

You don't need to hire anyone to check. Pull your last 20 closed deals or orders from the CRM or point of sale and trace each one back through the marketing touchpoints you can see. Do it by hand if you have to.

Say you trace 20 deals and find that 11 had a branded search click at the end, 7 of those 11 had earlier seen a paid social ad, and 4 came from a referral nobody tagged. That is already more useful than most monthly decks. The channel that keeps showing up early, even if it rarely gets last-click credit, is the one to protect when budgets get cut.

Then ask for an actual attribution report, not a case study. Ask what happens when platform numbers and CRM numbers don't match, and how often someone reconciles them. Monthly is common. Weekly is better. If it only happens at renewal time, the agency is producing a data-shaped report once a year to keep the account.

## Be patient with the build, not with the excuses

Real measurement takes time. The temptation is to skip to the dashboard and avoid the unglamorous work of connecting systems that were never designed to talk. An agency promising fully reconciled attribution on day one probably hasn't done the connecting yet.

Agree on definitions early, too. Plenty of agency-client friction comes from two sides using "attribution" or "performance" to mean different things. Write the definitions down in the first week.

## Five receipts to ask for

Whether you're in month two or month twenty with an agency, these five items separate a data-run program from a vibes-run one.

- **Revenue by channel.** Not leads, not traffic. Money.
- **Cost per customer, by campaign.** Cost per lead hides a lot of sins.
- **Proof the tracking works.** A recent test purchase or call traced end to end.
- **A test log.** What was tried, what happened, what changed because of it.
- **A written explanation of any gap** between platform numbers and CRM numbers.

An agency that can hand these over in a day has built the systems. One that needs a week and a lot of context is telling you something.

## Creative still matters

None of this is an argument against good creative. Strong creative is often the biggest lever in the account. The difference is that a data-driven agency can tell you which creative worked, for whom, and what it earned. Big ideas and a beautiful reel are fine. They just have to come with a measurement plan set before launch, not an explanation written afterward.

## The so-what

Judge your agency on receipts, not reports. Request last month's report and look for one number: revenue tied to a specific channel. If it's missing, ask why. That's a fair question for any agency, including a good one, and a good one will have an answer ready.
