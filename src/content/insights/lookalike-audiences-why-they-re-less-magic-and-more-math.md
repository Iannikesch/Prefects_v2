Lookalike audiences are among the most-hyped tools in paid social. Give the platform a list of your customers, and it finds people who resemble them. That sounds like magic. It's actually pattern-matching, and like any pattern-matching, the quality of the result depends on the quality of what you feed it.

## How it works

The process is simple. You provide a 'seed' audience, typically a customer list or a group of people who completed a valuable action. The platform analyzes the characteristics those people share, and then finds others in its user base who look similar. Different platforms call the feature different things and change its availability and controls over time, so check what your platform currently offers.

## The seed is everything

The seed is everything. A seed of all purchasers treats a customer who bought once on a heavy discount the same as one who has bought twelve times at full price. The platform can't tell the difference. It finds people who resemble your average buyer, and the average buyer may not be the one you want more of.

A better seed reflects value. Use your highest-lifetime-value customers, your repeat buyers, or customers who purchased without a discount. If you've identified who your best customers are, you can ask the platform to find more of them rather than more of everyone.

## An example

Consider a made-up case. An online home fitness retailer that runs two lookalike campaigns with the same budget and creative. One uses all 9,000 past purchasers as its seed. The other uses the 1,800 customers in the top fifth by 12-month revenue. In this hypothetical, the first campaign acquires customers at $71 each. The second acquires them at $58, and those customers spend about 20% more in their first year. Same platform, same budget, a better seed.

## Size, expansion and freshness

Size matters, but not the way you might expect. Platforms set minimum seed sizes, and very small seeds produce unreliable matches. Beyond that threshold, a larger seed isn't automatically better. A focused seed of 1,500 excellent customers can outperform a diluted one of 15,000 mixed ones. Balance quantity with quality, and follow your platform's current guidance.

Expansion settings control how closely the audience resembles the seed. A narrow lookalike, for instance the closest 1% of the population in a country, tends to be more similar but smaller. A broader one reaches more people at lower similarity. Start narrow when the budget is modest, test broader audiences as spending grows, and compare cost per customer, not just click rates.

Refresh the seed. Customers change, offers change, and a seed built two years ago may reflect a business that no longer exists. Update it at least quarterly, or automatically if your setup allows. A stale seed teaches the platform to find people who match yesterday's customers.

Platforms increasingly lean on automated audience expansion that can reach beyond a lookalike on its own. Where those features exist, a lookalike becomes more of a starting suggestion than a hard boundary. That doesn't make seed quality irrelevant. Signals you provide still shape where the system looks, so the same discipline applies.

## Creative, privacy and measurement

Remember that the audience is only half the equation. A well-built lookalike shown a weak ad still performs poorly, and a strong ad can carry a mediocre audience a surprising distance. Test creative within the lookalike campaign just as you would anywhere else, and keep existing customers excluded so the budget goes to new people, as covered in the earlier post on exclusions.

Privacy and consent apply. Customer lists used as seeds should be collected and used in line with your privacy policy and applicable law. Hashing data before upload is standard practice, but it doesn't remove your obligations. Make sure your customers' data is being used in ways they'd reasonably expect.

Don't skip measurement. Compare lookalike campaigns against other audience approaches, such as interest targeting or broad targeting, using the same creative and budget over the same period. Judge by cost per qualified customer and by downstream value. Sometimes broad targeting with strong creative beats a carefully built lookalike, and the only way to know is to test.

Test lookalikes against a simple control. Run a broad-targeting campaign with the same creative alongside, and compare cost per customer over at least a few weeks. Platforms increasingly find good prospects on their own, and a lookalike earns its place only if it beats the simpler setup. Letting the data pick the winner beats arguing about which approach sounds more sophisticated.

Regional seeds need care. A lookalike built from customers in one country and aimed at another can perform oddly, since buying behavior and platform user bases differ. Where possible, build seeds from customers in the market you're targeting, and keep markets separate so results stay interpretable.

## The so-what

A lookalike audience is a way of asking a platform to find more people like the ones you already value. The quality of the answer depends on the quality of the question: a seed built from your best customers, refreshed regularly, and tested against alternatives. Fed a mixed bag, it returns a mixed bag.
