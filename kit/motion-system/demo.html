How to read any motion

First ask what makes it move. That's the trigger, and it names the whole family.

A · Time

Ambient loop

Runs on a clock whether you do anything or not. Here: aurora, globe, nebula, both marquees.

B · Scroll position

Scroll-scrubbed

Tied to how far you've scrolled. Stop scrolling and it stops; scroll back and it rewinds. Here: parallax, word reveal, card lag.

C · Scroll event

Scroll-triggered

Fires once when something enters view, then plays on its own clock. Here: the footer heading.

D · Pointer

Pointer-driven

Follows the mouse. Here: the cursor ring. Hover effects would also live here, and Profectus has none.

01 · Smoothing layer

Smooth scroll

Also called inertial scrolling · momentum scrolling · scroll smoothing · "Lenis"

How it works

Your scroll wheel doesn't move the page directly. It sets a target, and every frame the page covers 13.5% of the distance left to it. Big steps first, then smaller and smaller, so it glides to a stop instead of jumping. Profectus uses a library called Lenis for this, and it's running on this page now.

Use it when

You have scroll-scrubbed effects (07–09). Smoothing makes them glide instead of stutter. It also sets the calm, expensive tone before anything else moves.

Avoid

Making it slow or floaty; that reads as lag. Never change how far a scroll goes or snap to sections with it.

02 · Ambient loop

Colour cycling

Also called hue flow · gradient animation · aurora gradient

How it works

The ring never moves. The colours travel along it, clockwise over the top: violet appears on the left, crosses the crown and exits right. The trick is a colour wheel (a conic gradient) turning slowly behind a ring-shaped cut-out. The left panel shows the wheel; the right shows what the cut-out lets through. When the pale "mist" part of the wheel passes the visible arc, the whole glow seems to breathe brighter.

Use it when

A hero or closing section needs life without distracting from the headline. It is light, not paint.

Avoid

Speeding it up; under 15 seconds a cycle it starts to look like a loading spinner. One per screen.

03 · Ambient loop

Continuous rotation

Also called idle spin · auto-rotate · turntable

How it works

A dotted globe turns at a constant speed and never stops. It's drawn one dot at a time, so the dots near the edge dim and the surface looks curved. Profectus renders it in WebGL; this is a lighter 2D canvas version. The continents here are rough, simplified shapes, not an accurate map.

Use it when

An object stands for something big and ongoing ("global reach", "always on"). Slow and steady reads as confident.

Avoid

Spinning faster than about one turn every 30s, or letting the user drag it unless dragging does something useful.

04 · Ambient loop

Flow field

Also called noise drift · particle flow · Perlin field

How it works

Thousands of specks drift along invisible currents. Every point on the screen has a direction (the "field"), and the field slowly shifts over time, so the specks trace faint strands that never repeat. On Profectus it sits behind "Why Profectus" and runs down into the footer at 37% opacity. Its overall shape barely changes; the motion is a fine shimmer inside the strands.

Use it when

A long section needs texture that feels alive but asks for no attention at all.

Avoid

Raising the opacity until it competes with text. If you notice it while reading, it's too strong.

05 · Ambient loop

Infinite marquee

Also called ticker · logo loop · scrolling banner · crawl

How it works

A row of items slides left at a constant speed. The row is duplicated, so when the first copy has slid fully out, the second copy is exactly where the first started and the loop restarts invisibly. It pauses when scrolled off screen to save battery. It doesn't pause on hover.

Use it when

You have many equal items (clients, industries, logos) and want to show breadth without a grid.

Avoid

Putting anything in it that must be read or clicked. People read at their own pace, not the marquee's.

06 · Ambient loop

Stepped carousel

Also called auto-advancing carousel · autoplay slider · step-and-hold

How it works

It looks like a marquee, but it isn't one. It holds still for about two seconds, jumps exactly one item to the left in 0.6s, and holds again. The jump starts fast and brakes hard (expo-out easing), so it feels decisive. Holding still is what lets each number be read.

Use it when

Items need to be read one at a time: stats, quotes, results. That's the difference from 05.

Avoid

Holds shorter than about 2 seconds, or a bouncy easing. Both make numbers hard to read.

07 · Scroll-scrubbed

Parallax

Also called parallax scrolling · scroll-linked translate · depth scrolling

How it works

The photo behind the band moves at a different speed from the page, so it seems to sit further back. Profectus starts it only when the band begins leaving through the top of the screen (its "exit"). Over the band's own height the photo slides 133px, from 60px up to 73px down. The photo is enlarged 1.2× so its edges never show.

Use it when

A full-width photo band needs depth. Keep the ratio small; Profectus moves the photo about one pixel for every five scrolled.

Avoid

Parallax on text, or big ratios. Both make pages feel seasick.

08 · Scroll-scrubbed

Scroll-scrubbed text reveal

Also called word-by-word reveal · text scrub · scroll-driven highlight

How it works

Every word starts at 20% opacity. As you scroll, the words fill in one at a time, as if the page is reading along with you. Each word takes 100px of scroll to fill, and the next starts 50px after the one before, so about two words are filling at any moment. Scroll back up and they dim again.

Use it when

A single statement headline should be read slowly and land with weight. Once per page.

Avoid

Body copy, and forgetting reduced motion. The source leaves the words stuck at 20% for users who turn motion off; this version shows them in full.

09 · Scroll-scrubbed + spring

Spring-smoothed parallax

Also called lagged parallax · smooth follow · inertia parallax

How it works

Like 07, each card's position depends on scroll, but a spring sits in between. The card doesn't jump to where scroll says it should be; it's pulled there, as if on a soft elastic band, and arrives a moment late. Give each card a different pull and the grid loosens as you scroll, then settles. Profectus's spring is tuned so it never overshoots (no bounce).

Use it when

A grid of cards should feel physical and loose rather than locked to the page.

Avoid

Bouncy springs (low damping); they feel like a toy. Keep the pull differences small.

10 · Pointer-driven

Cursor follower

Also called custom cursor · trailing cursor · cursor ring

How it works

A thin ring with a violet dot chases your pointer on a spring, the same kind of spring as 09 but stiffer. Move fast and it trails behind, then catches up and settles without bouncing. It's on for this whole page; the normal cursor stays visible, so nothing is lost. It hides on touch screens.

Use it when

A site wants one small, constant signal of craft. It's cheap to run.

Avoid

Hiding the real cursor, or a ring that changes size on every hover. Profectus's ring doesn't react to anything.

11 · Scroll-triggered

Fade-up reveal

Also called reveal on scroll · entrance animation · fade-in-up

How it works

The heading starts invisible and 48px low. When it scrolls into view it rises and fades in once, then stays. Unlike 08 it doesn't follow the scroll; scrolling only starts it. It's the only reveal on the whole Profectus page, on the footer's "Ready to Make Progress?".

Use it when

A closing call to action should arrive rather than just be there. Sparingly.

Avoid

Putting it on everything. A page where every block fades up feels like a template.

12 · What Profectus leaves still

Restraint is part of the system. These are measured absences: don't add them back by accident.

No load-in

No intro animation

Everything is fully visible 20ms after the page appears. No preloader, no page transition, no staggered hero entrance.

No hover motion

No hover states

Buttons, links and cards don't react to the pointer at all, even with the browser's hover state confirmed active. Hover effects exist in the page's code but never run.

Instant toggle

No menu transition

The mobile menu opens in one frame and closes in one frame. No slide, no fade.

Split, but still

No letter animation

Two headlines are split into single letters, the usual setup for a letter-by-letter animation, but neither animates.

Glossary · the words you'll meet

Easing
How speed changes during a movement. Linear is constant speed; most natural motion isn't.
Ease-out
Starts fast, slows into place. The default for anything arriving. Profectus's steps (06) and word fills (08) use it.
Cubic-bezier
Four numbers that draw an easing curve, e.g. cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1). Paste them into cubic-bezier.com to see the shape.
Spring
Motion driven by physics instead of a fixed duration. Stiffness is how hard it pulls, damping is how much it resists. High damping means no bounce ("overdamped"), which is how both Profectus springs are tuned.
Lerp
Short for linear interpolation: each frame, move a fixed share of the remaining distance. Smooth scroll (01) is a lerp of 0.135.
Scrub
Tying an animation's progress to scroll position instead of time, like dragging a video's playhead.
Trigger
What starts a movement: time, scroll position, a scroll event, or the pointer. Name the trigger first and you've named the family.
Stagger
A delay between siblings doing the same thing, so they go one after another. In 08 the stagger is measured in scroll pixels, not milliseconds.
Ambient
Motion that runs on its own in the background and asks for no attention.
Exit range
The stretch of scrolling during which an element leaves the screen. Parallax (07) only runs in this range.
Reduced motion
A system setting people turn on because motion makes them unwell. Every mover here stops when it's on. The source only stops one.
rAF · WAAPI · Lenis
The engines: requestAnimationFrame (a script redraws every frame), the Web Animations API (the browser runs a keyframed animation itself), and Lenis (the smooth-scroll library).
